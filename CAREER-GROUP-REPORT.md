## Balance verdict

**Promising, but not fully balanced.**

- Experience clearly changes development speed. New players typically take 74–127 weeks on a committed schedule, while racket and competitive starters reach a pro event around week 27.
- Every committed cohort eventually reaches a professional event. That makes the strongest progression variable time rather than success.
- Light schedules are more selective, with pro-event rates mainly between 91% and 99%. The spread by experience is real but narrow.
- Ten-year workload is bounded around 650–685 matches on the light schedule and roughly 780–870 on the committed schedule; Quick Sim keeps this playable.
- Age differentiation is visible in the light group's rating outcome, especially at 40 and 50, but committed schedules compress the difference.
- No sampled player career reached the PPA Finals, so top-eight qualification remains an unresolved progression endpoint.
- CPU age 20 has a fast-track anomaly: active 20-year-olds receive a pro invitation at median week 8, compared with week 27 for ages 25–30. Success remains 61%, but the timing is too early.

## Recommended tuning

1. Set explicit committed pro-debut targets below 100% for new/casual backgrounds, especially older starters.
2. Delay the youth CPU fast track so age 20 does not bypass the 25–30 pathway.
3. Increase late-career performance separation without reducing technique growth.
4. Review the Overall ranking and finals cut so strong careers can reach the top eight.
5. Re-run this report after tuning and compare distributions, not only averages.
