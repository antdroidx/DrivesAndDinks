Split the rating by format, open up the brackets, and drop the seasons

A player is now rated separately in singles, doubles and mixed doubles, and
each format has its own standing. The three diverge because the games reward
different things: doubles leans on the soft game and the net, singles on the
serve and the feet, and mixed is a blend. Every athlete carries a bias
toward one of them, and the population plays both formats in the world
simulation.

Events carry the formats they run and the brackets they field. Some events
are completely open, with no rating gates at all, which is what ladder events
and open tournaments are for: a player who feels underrated can go and play
better company, and anyone can drop into a lower bracket to grind. A gated
event only takes players whose rating sits in the bracket, with one
allowance, because a bracket half a step above their own is always open to
them. A 3.3 player can enter a 3.5 tournament.

There are no seasons. Tournaments run all year, leagues and the amateur MLP
run for a month or three, the pro tour runs about nine months, and the MLP is
a summer circuit. The ladder gained the two MLP events to match.

Training moves attributes only, by a single point, and never the rating.
Anything that gives you a rating is an event. Playing an event moves the
rating in the format entered and grows one attribute, weighted by the skill
the event rewards, what the player is strongest at, and what the format asks
for.

A career can now move to the other region, for a higher energy cost. The
pro circuit is international, so the question was never whether a player can
go, only what it costs.

Also fixes the club night button, which said PRACTISE for what is a rated
match, and the new career button, which had no action behind it.
